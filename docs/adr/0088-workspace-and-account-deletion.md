## Context

Nobody could delete a workspace. Deleting an account deleted the workspaces where the person was the only member at once, inside the request, with only a session cookie as proof: a stolen or unattended session wiped an account and its workspaces, machine included, in one call. The web app had no way to delete an account, though the Terms and the Privacy Policy point to Settings. Two ways around a stronger proof were open: a session could mint a fresh session (the phone's one-time token, the desktop app's redeem), and it could add a password to an account that had none and then present it.

A deleted workspace also left rows behind: tables with no foreign key to it (reactions, routing decisions, reflections, context usage), usage rows with no agent, and wiki links. "Closed" was a check on `suspended` repeated at a dozen entry points. Deleting a person or an agent scanned every partition of `messages`.

## Decision

### 1. Owners delete a workspace: it turns read-only now and goes 7 days later

- **Who.** Any owner, never an admin. From Settings › General on the web and the desktop app, and from the onboarding, which is the way out of a workspace made by mistake. The phone's row opens that web page. The dialog asks for the workspace's name, typed or pasted, compared after NFKC normalization with Unicode format characters removed, its spaces collapsed, trimmed and in any case (`matchesName`, which deleting an agent uses too), so a co-owner can't make it untypeable with invisible characters, and a name of nothing but those needs nothing typed. The server doesn't take the name: the proof (§2) is its guard.
- **The route.** `POST /api/app/organizations/{orgId}/deletion` with `{ code?, password? }` (§2) answers 202 `{ deletion: { dueAt } }`. It refuses anyone but a member (404 once the workspace is gone), then a suspended workspace (`org.suspended`), then anyone but an owner (`forbidden`). One already being deleted answers 202 with its date, before the proof and the daily limit, and nobody is told again. Then the proof, then at most 3 deletions of a workspace a day (`org-deletion:{orgId}`, 429), counted only once the proof passed.
- **At once, in one transaction** under the workspace's `org-members` lock, the role read again: a conditional update sets `deletion_due_at` to now plus 7 days (`WORKSPACE_DELETION_DAYS`), `deletion_requested_at` and `deletion_requested_by`, only while none is set, so two owners at once or a double click keep one date. A computer wait that isn't ready is dropped, as for a hold. The workspace's audit log gets `organization.deletion_scheduled` (`dueAt`, `via`), the operator's feed `organization.deletion_requested`, each member an email (§7), and the outbox `org.delete` at the date (no `org_id`, so the workspace's cascade leaves it; deduplicated per workspace and date).
- **After the commit:** the cached standing goes, members' apps hear `org.deleting` (`orgId`, `dueAt`, `by: { id, name }`) on sockets that stay open *(amended 2026-11-11; they closed with 4403 before)*, and its work stops (`stopOrgWork`): runs and computer tasks are canceled, its agents' desktops end, and a routine's run closes as `org_deleting`.
- **Every deletion takes the 7 days,** the onboarding's and an account's own workspaces (§10) included. There is no "delete now".

### 2. Proof that it's you

*Amended 2026-10-31: one emailed code replaces both a sign-in in the last 10 minutes and the password, which stays only where the server sends no email.*

- **A code emailed to the person** proves it, for every account, on every server that sends email (MillionSend or SMTP). `POST /api/app/organizations/{orgId}/deletion/code` (owners, refused as the deletion is) and `POST /api/app/account/deletion/code` (`sole_owner` first, as the deletion is) answer 202 `{ email, resendIn }`: 6 digits to the account's own address, in its language, which say what goes (the workspace by name, or the account by its address) and, if it wasn't them, not to share it and whom to write to (`SUPPORT_EMAIL`, else the people who run the server). The deletion sends it as `{ code }`.
- **Its rules are every emailed code's** (`email-codes.ts`, which asking to join uses too, ADR 0072): it works for 10 minutes, and once; a new one replaces it, 60 s after the last at the soonest (429 `reauth.code_wait`, `params.retryIn`); 5 an hour and 10 a day per account (429 `reauth.code_limit`); 5 wrong tries use it up (409 `reauth.code_tries`), and an account gets 10 wrong tries a day, counted in a `verifications` row of its own (`deletion-code:{userId}:wrong`): past them every code is refused unread and none is sent until that day ends (429 `reauth.code_limit`), so a guess past them learns nothing and no email carries a code that can't work. It is bound to the person and to what it deletes: one Better Auth `verifications` row per person and deletion (`deletion-code:{userId}:workspace:{orgId}` or `…:account`) holding only its hash and its tries, so a code sent for anything else, or to anyone else, is wrong (400 `reauth.code_wrong`). Without one: 403 `reauth.code`; past its 10 minutes: 409 `reauth.code_expired`. The code is never logged: its email's outbox row goes once it is sent or fails for good, as a join code's does, and rows a day past their end go at the hourly `deletions` tick. Where email goes, a password proves nothing here.
- **Where the server sends no email** (a self-hosted install with neither `MILLIONSEND_API_KEY` nor `SMTP_URL`), the code routes answer 409 `reauth.no_email` and the deletion takes `{ password }` instead: checked by Better Auth, at most 5 tries in 15 minutes per account (`deletion-proof:{userId}`, 429), counted before the check. Without it or wrong: 403 `reauth.password`. An account without a password adds one first.
- **Adding a password** (`POST /api/app/me/password`) still needs a sign-in in the last 10 minutes (403 `reauth.sign_in`), checked before `password_set`: a password signs in from anywhere, and where no email goes it is the proof.
- **A session minted from another keeps that one's sign-in time:** the phone's one-time token when a session mints it, and the desktop app's redeem, so neither can stand in for that recent sign-in, and the desktop hand-off's own 10-minute check means a real sign-in. One minted for the owner key (ADR 0066) starts fresh, since the key is a credential.
- **Better Auth's own password routes stay off over HTTP** (`disabledPaths`: `/verify-password`, `/change-password`). Each told any session whether a password was right, with no limit but the sign-in service's own per IP address, so a stolen session could have guessed the password there, to sign in with it anywhere or delete with it where no email goes. No app called them; the proof calls `verifyPassword` in the process, and a forgotten password is reset by email.
- Restoring needs no proof: it destroys nothing.

### 3. Closed is one predicate

- `closedRefusal(standing)` (`standing.ts`) is why a workspace is closed to every change and all work: suspended (`org.suspended`, the operator's, which comes first), else being deleted (`OrgDeletingError`: 403 `org.deleting`, not retryable, `params.dueAt` while the date is ahead), else null. `workRefusal` adds holds; `closedOrg` and `openOrg` say the same in SQL for queries over `organizations`.
- **Reading stays open while it is being deleted** *(amended 2026-11-11)*. `readRefusal(standing)` refuses only a suspension, and it alone guards every permission check and channel access, the socket's upgrade and subscriptions, brain settings and usage: a workspace being deleted reads as ever for its members. Its changes are refused in one place, `readOnlyWhileDeleting` (`apps/server/src/routes/app/context.ts`, before every route under `/api/app/organizations/{orgId}`): any request but `GET` and `HEAD` answers `closedRefusal`'s refusal to a member, except restoring or deleting it (`…/deletion`, `…/deletion/code`), leaving it (`DELETE …/members/{me}`), what they read (`…/channels/{id}/read`, `…/threads/{id}/read`), their inbox (`…/notifications/…`), their own settings there (`…/me…`, `…/notification-prefs`), opening a DM (`POST …/dms`: `openDm` opens one it has as it is, bringing nobody back into it, and refuses a new one) and the payer's way to its invoices and card (`POST …/billing-link`). Anyone else gets what the route says to them, never that it is being deleted. A suspended workspace is left to the routes, which refuse it everything. Writes into it from outside that path never reach that middleware, so each refuses it itself with `closedRefusal`: an OAuth return (a connector's, the GitHub App's install, a mailbox's sign-in), driving a browser profile (`requireProfileDriver`, which the socket's `browser.input` checks too; `requireChangePermission` in `organizations.ts` is the permission check for these), a person's message (`postPersonMessage`, which a voice note's job posts through), accepting an invitation and an AI app's consent and calls. The jobs that would write in it do nothing: GitHub's news (`handleGitHubAppEvent`), skills that update by themselves, the team's hello and the team's week.
- It closes desktop and terminal streams (closed with the reason `deleting`), browser hand-offs, context tools, the machine device check, invitations (sending, links, an email invitation's page, which reads as closed as a link's does, the logo on an invitation's page, asking for a new one, which tells nobody, and joining, which reads the row again under the `org-members` lock, so nobody gets in once a deletion committed), sign-ups through an invitation to it, and the work gate (`standingRefusal`: the Model Gateway, the hub, the executor, the judge and the router; a routine's slot and a message the router passes over are skipped as `org_deleting`). A refused run reads `run.org_deleting`, with nothing to contest. The onboarding doesn't resume it (`unfinishedWorkspaces`) and starts a new workspace, and a server's first account doesn't become its owner (`onUserCreated`).
- `GET /me`, restoring and leaving stay open, and reading while it is being deleted.
- A test fails when code outside `standing.ts` compares a workspace's standing to a state.
- **A deletion is never a standing.** It lives in its own columns: setting a standing neither reads nor clears it, and the standing in `GET /me`, `org.standing` and the operator API doesn't carry it.

### 4. Restore, before the date only

- `DELETE /api/app/organizations/{orgId}/deletion` (204): any owner, with no proof. 409 `org.not_restorable` when it isn't being deleted or its date passed; `org.suspended` while suspended; `org.create_unpaid` (`params.orgId`) when it waits for its first plan and the person restoring owns another that does, under the same lock as creating a workspace (ADR 0079 §2); 404 once it is gone.
- Under the `org-members` lock it clears the three columns, writes `organization.deletion_canceled` in the audit log and the feed, and tells every member by email (§7). The operator's deletion hold (§5) is never touched. There is no broadcast: the line on top of the workspace (§7) asks `GET /me` again. Work canceled while it was closed stays canceled, and routines fire again at their next time.
- **The date is the point of no return,** by the database's clock: restoring works only before it and the purge only after it. Each checks it once it holds the workspace's `org-members` lock (the purge's first look too, before anything it can't undo), by the clock of that statement, not of its transaction, which may have waited for the lock: so neither can undo the other.

### 5. Holds make the purge wait, and never refuse a deletion

- A workspace past its date is **kept** (`keptFromDeletion`) while it is suspended, on a hold that isn't billing's, or under the operator's **deletion hold**. Billing holds (ADR 0079) never keep one, `ended` included.
- Kept never refuses: an owner deletes a workspace under a trust hold or a deletion hold, it turns read-only at once, and its purge waits until the hold lifts. Nothing the workspace reads says why: past its date `GET /me` gives `dueAt: null`, and the apps say only that it is being deleted.
- **The deletion hold** is `PUT /api/operator/v1/orgs/{orgId}/deletion-hold` with `{ held, reason? }` (ADR 0056 §9), set with a legal hold. It refuses nothing and tells the workspace nothing, and restoring never lifts it, so a workspace deleted again later still waits.

### 6. The purge: the `org.delete` job

1. Under the `org-members` lock, it reads the workspace past its date that nothing keeps, and does nothing when it was restored, is kept or is gone.
2. **AI accounts leave its computers** (ADR 0059 §1): every one becomes a tombstone, and the purge waits up to 60 seconds for its online computers whose runner removes accounts. An offline computer keeps them, and its token dies with the workspace.
3. **Outside grants are revoked while their sealed copies still exist:** Composio forgets its mailboxes (their triggers, and the connected accounts, which revokes them at Google or Microsoft unless another connection still in use reaches the same mailbox, since they share that grant), and GitHub its OAuth tokens, where the GitHub OAuth app is configured (each connection's token, never the whole grant, which would also cut off the same GitHub account's connections in other workspaces; disconnecting one does the same). A 404 counts as done, and each is marked as it goes, so a retry skips it. A failure fails the job, which retries; its final attempt goes on and logs the ids it couldn't revoke, never a token. GitHub App installations stay installed, as unbinding leaves them, and their cached tokens are forgotten. Other connectors' tokens, keys, IMAP passwords and database credentials go with their rows, since their providers can't be told.
4. **One transaction:** the `org-members` lock, the row `for update`, and the check again, so a hold that committed first wins. It deletes the workspace's rows from the tables nothing cascades to (`PURGED_BY_HAND`: `message_reactions`, `routing_decisions`, `thread_reflections`, `usage_records`, `wiki_links` and `context_usage`, by their `org_id`, so a run deleted earlier leaves nothing behind), records `organization.deleted` (`userId`: who asked, null once their account is gone; `requestedAt`, and `via` from the workspace's own `organization.deletion_scheduled` row), queues the files' purge (ADR 0019), and deletes the row, which cascades.
5. **After the commit:** each of its computers gets `machine.revoke`, and the server logs `workspace deleted` (`orgId`, `requestedBy`, `via`), the only trace on a server without the operator API.

- **No orphans.** A test reads the migrated schema: every table with `org_id` cascades from its workspace (directly, or through a chain of cascading keys on columns never null), is in `PURGED_BY_HAND`, or is kept on purpose (`operator_events`, ADR 0056 §5; `organization_join_code_events`, gone after a day, ADR 0072). A new table that is none of these fails it.
- **The hourly sweep.** The workers' `deletions` tick, hourly and at boot, queues `org.delete` for every workspace past its date that nothing keeps, at most once an hour each: a hold lifted after the date, a job that failed for good, one a deploy lost.

### 7. What people see and are told

- `GET /me` adds, while a workspace is being deleted, `organizations[].deletion: { dueAt, requestedAt, requestedBy: { id, name } | null }`, with `dueAt` null once the date passed.
- *(Amended 2026-11-11.)* The apps open the workspace as ever, read-only, under one line on top of the work area (on the phone, on top of Conversas): when it goes for good (only that it is going, once the date passed), who deleted it (the person who did reads that they did), and Restore for owners before the date (members read that only an owner can restore it). Its message boxes (a profile card's and a notice's quick reply too) say why nothing can be sent, any other change is refused in the person's words, and Settings › General has no delete card. Settings greys out each section that would change it (`SETTINGS_OPEN_WHILE_DELETING` in `packages/client/src/lib/deletion.ts` lists those that stay: the person's own, what only reads, the Vault, roles, skills, the agents' access, import and export, and billing), no new agent is offered (`makesAgents`: nor an import of agents, where the export stays) and no new connection (`addsConnectors`), a board offers no change (`frozenBoard`: no Organize, "+", Rename, Share, Pin or Delete, and its cards only open), and no routine, reminder or widget shows a time it won't run at. A refusal says its reason wherever the person acted, with no Try again where trying can't change it. Its other notices (a hold, the computer on its way, a computer offline) step aside: its work stopped, nothing else is news. Switching workspaces and making a new one stay in the usual menus. A suspension still takes the workspace's place, the stronger fact. Nobody hears of a restore, so the line asks `GET /me` again every 30 seconds and on focus: a restore takes it away, and a deleted workspace leaves the list.
- **Emails,** only where the server sends email, each queued with no `org_id` so the cascade leaves it (ADR 0069: our words in the subject and the buttons, the workspace's and the deleter's names inert in the body):
  - `workspace_deleting`, one per member (blocked accounts aside), with the date and time in the member's time zone, else the workspace's. Owners get a button to restore it, the others read to ask an owner. Never for an account's own workspaces, which `account_deleted` names.
  - `workspace_restored`, one per member.
  - No email at the purge, and no notification or push: the workspace's work stopped, and the line and the email carry it. People with pending invitations or requests to join aren't written to: an invitation link and an email invitation read as closed and accepting one is refused, and after the purge neither is found. Once the workspace is deleted for good, the app leaves for the person's start.

### 8. The workspace's computers

- **Self-hosted**, at the purge: AI sign-ins leave online computers first, then every computer gets `machine.revoke`, and its runner stops for good and says it was removed. The agents' folders and OS users stay until the runner is uninstalled, as when a computer is removed. The server's own computer loses that workspace's row, and its next sync drops it.
- **External provisioning** (ADR 0056 §10): a workspace being deleted is no computer request, and a managed computer that says hello meanwhile isn't made ready for it, nor announced to its owners; a restore brings the computer back, which says hello again. On agentes.chat Cloud the operator's services read the feed: they hold the machine during the 7 days as they hold one whose plan ended (its runner and agents stopped, its network cut, its disk kept), so a restore is whole; they release at once a machine still being prepared; they bring it back at a restore; and they release it at `organization.deleted`.

### 9. Files, search, early access, billing and audit

- **Files:** platform storage through `org.files.purge`, two passes (ADR 0019); files in the database cascade. A workspace's own bucket is left alone, and the dialog says so. Photos are per person and stay.
- **Search** is Postgres only, so it cascades. A search backend that keeps text outside Postgres must drop the workspace in the purge, before its row goes.
- **Early access:** the invitation's workspace goes null at the purge, and the allowance stays, so the person can make a new workspace.
- **Billing:** the server keeps no billing state. A billing service reads the three events: on agentes.chat Cloud the plan stops renewing at the request, renews again at a restore, and is canceled after `organization.deleted`. *Amended 2026-10-31:* nothing is refunded at all, not even part of a period, and the dialog says so where `billingUrl` is set. A billing service also reads `user.deleted`: on the Cloud every plan the account paid for stops renewing, the workspaces it leaves included, since nobody else can stop a renewal on that card, and a restore doesn't renew it. The account's dialog says so too where `billingUrl` is set. *Amended 2026-11-05:* it reads `user.deletion_requested` the same way, so no plan renews during the 7 days, and `organization.deletion_canceled` with `data.via = "account"` renews nothing: restoring an account charges nothing until the person turns a plan back on. Control deploys before core for these (Cloud `docs/runbook.md`).
- **Audit:** the workspace's log keeps `organization.deletion_scheduled` and `organization.deletion_canceled` until it goes; the operator's feed keeps the three events for 90 days; the server's log keeps the line of the purge.

### 10. Deleting an account

- **Where.** "Delete account" at the bottom of Settings › Your account on the web and the desktop app, a danger card like the one that ends Settings › General for owners, never in the account menu beside Sign out, where a slip would open it; and on the phone's account page, native, as the stores require. Where those settings don't show and the person has no workspace that opens, the screen itself has it: a workspace being deleted or suspended, and the onboarding, on the web; on the phone the closing and suspended screens open the account page over them, and the first run opens the web's. Both read the preflight, `GET /api/app/account/deletion`: `{ closes, leaves, soleOwner, computers, staysBehind, serverOwner }`, which the page shows before anyone confirms: blockers with the way out of each (a workspace still in its onboarding is finished or deleted there), or what closes (one being deleted already keeps its own date, `closes[].deletion.dueAt`), what they leave, the computers removed, what stays behind with a link to each one's settings to disconnect it first, where `billingUrl` is set that the plans they pay for stop renewing with nothing refunded (§9), that their messages stay without their name, and the proof (§2): Send code, then the code; or the password where no email goes, which the website adds first to an account without one. A phone build reaching a server from before the preflight (404) shows its fixed lines and deletes as it always did.
- **`DELETE /api/app/account`** takes `{ code?, password? }` (§2) and answers 204 with the session cookies expired. It refuses, in order: the only owner of a workspace with others in it that isn't being deleted (409 `sole_owner`, `params.organizations` with the names and `orgIds`; checked again under the locks), the proof (§2), and a blocked account (403). Make someone else an owner, or delete that workspace, and it no longer blocks.
- **In one transaction**, first under the lock creating a workspace takes for them (so one made in another tab meanwhile is in the plan and closes with the account, or finds the account gone), then under every workspace's `org-members` lock in a fixed order, writing every workspace row before the user row, as the purge does (`accountDeletionPlan` reads what follows):
  - workspaces only they are in close as in §1 (`via: "account"`), whatever their standing: a hold makes their purge wait, never the account. One already being deleted keeps its date;
  - their AI accounts on every computer become tombstones before the user row goes (ADR 0059 §1), and every workspace's computers hear it, left and closing;
  - each workspace they leave records `member.left` with `accountDeleted: true`;
  - their personal computers in the workspaces they leave are removed (§11);
  - the `account_deleted` email, queued before the user row because it needs their address, language and time zone: the workspaces it closed and their date, and whom to write to if it wasn't them (`SUPPORT_EMAIL`, else the people who run the server);
  - the user row, and with it their sessions on every device, sign-in methods, phones, memberships, channel memberships, notifications and requests to join;
  - the pinned owner's Jev key, and the pin once no account is left (ADR 0073 §7), `user.deleted` in the feed, and `user.files.purge` for their photos (ADR 0019).
- **After the commit:** `membership.revoked` in every workspace closes their sockets, each workspace that closed is announced and its work stops, the removed computers are told, and Apple's tokens are revoked (ADR 0035).
- **Stays:** messages and records they wrote, without an author (Privacy Policy §4); audit rows that name them; agents they were responsible for, with no responsible person; routines they made, which pause at their next firing (`creator_left`); the keys, connections and mailboxes they added, which are the workspace's, listed in the preflight so they can disconnect one first; the feed (90 days); the waiting-list entry and the early-access invitation, whose row keeps the address out of the next batch of invitations.
- **Their other tabs and devices.** A cached session cookie outlives the account for up to 5 minutes: `GET /me` answers 401 and expires the cookies once the user row is gone, and creating a workspace locks the user row and answers 401 for a missing one.
- **The pinned owner may delete their account** (ADR 0073 §7). The preflight says the server's own settings will then have no owner here, only in its environment (`serverOwner`). Until no account is left, the owner key signs in nobody and the server's own computer serves no workspace.

- **One thing's screen never offers to delete another** *(amended 2026-11-05)*. The suspended screen and the onboarding offer Delete account only to someone with no workspace that opens (`accountOutOfReach`; one being deleted opens), who has no other way to Settings › Your account, and then under its own "Your whole account" heading, never beside Restore or Sign out. Everyone else goes to their other workspace, the screen's main action. The dialog's title and first line, and the code's email, say it's the whole account. `GET /account/deletion` gives each workspace that would close its `agents` and `messages` (`workspaceContents`, outside `accountDeletionPlan` and its locks, messages counted per conversation on the index as `orgMessageCount` does), and the dialog links to deleting just one workspace. Lists say a closed workspace is closed, and tell apart two with the same name by state, else by `createdAt` (`workspaceNotes`).

*Amended 2026-11-05: an account's deletion waits 7 days, like a workspace's. What this section calls the transaction and its after-commit is now what the `account.delete` job does at the date.*

- **`DELETE /api/app/account`** keeps its refusals and its proof, and calls `scheduleAccountDeletion`: `users.deletion_due_at` and `deletion_requested_at` (migration `0230_account_deletion`), all sessions and phones gone, `user.deletion_requested` in the feed, the `account_deleting` email (when, what closes, how to come back), `account.delete` in the outbox at the date. Workspaces only they are in close in the same transaction, so their date is the account's; one being deleted already keeps its own. It answers 204 with the cookies expired. Twice keeps the date.
- **While it waits** (`accountInUse` in `standing.ts`): every membership reads as none, so no workspace, socket, DM, app token, invitation, request to join or new workspace answers it, and `GET /me` gives `user.deletion: { dueAt, requestedAt }` with no workspaces. It leaves `listMembers` (people, mentions, assignees) and the owner counts, so the last owner in use can't leave or delete past it (`sole_owner`). `queueEmail` drops its address except for password resets, email confirmations and the two emails about the deletion. Its memberships, sign-in methods, computers and agents stay as they are, so a person with a password or with Google, GitHub or Apple signs in as always and lands on the waiting screen: one screen in place of the app (`AccountDeletingScreen`, `DeletingAccount`) with the date, what it means, Restaurar and Sair. Routines it made pass their slots and keep their schedule, so a restore finds them running. An approval named to it goes to who can answer (`approverIds`). Its connected apps get no token, and none is revoked: a restore finds them connected.
- **`DELETE /api/app/account/deletion`** (204; 409 `account.not_restorable` past the date or when nothing waits) clears the columns and brings back the workspaces that closed with it, whatever their member's role, telling nobody. One that can't come back yet stays being deleted, read-only under its line, where Restore says why (a suspended one stays on its screen). `user.deletion_canceled` goes in the feed. The date is the point of no return as in §4, by the same clock under the lock `deleteAccount` takes first.
- **`account.delete`** (`purgeAccount`) does what this section describes, then revokes Apple's tokens: a no-op once restored or gone. A blocked account waits, and the `deletions` tick queues what is overdue once an hour.
- **The same address during the 7 days** belongs to this account: signing up with it is refused as for any taken address, and signing in shows the waiting screen. It is free once the account is gone.

### 11. A person's own computers leave with them

When a person leaves a workspace, is removed from it or deletes their account, the computers they connected there go with them (`personalMachines`: `connected` computers they paired, not in system mode), removed as any computer is: their agents are left with no computer, the runner hears `machine.revoke`, and the audit row names who left or who removed them. Every agent there runs as that person's OS user (ADR 0058), so nobody else's agents may go on running as a former member. A system-mode computer is the team's server and stays, and the server's own computer and a managed one are nobody's. Their AI accounts become tombstones first (ADR 0059 §1).

### 12. Limits

3 deletions of a workspace a day, a deletion code's limits (§2), 5 password tries in 15 minutes per account where no email goes, and 10 new workspaces a day per person (`POST /api/app/organizations`, 429, every request counted), so a loop of deleting and restoring can't email everyone all day, and one of creating and deleting can't churn the Cloud's computers.

### 13. Data

One migration, `0138_workspace_deletion`: `organizations.deletion_due_at`, `deletion_requested_at`, `deletion_requested_by` (a user, set null when that account goes), `deletion_hold_at` and `deletion_hold_reason` (the operator's own, never shown), with no backfill and no index, since the sweep reads a small table; and the two author indexes on `messages` (ADR 0009).

## Consequences

- An owner's deletion can be undone for 7 days by any owner, except while the workspace is suspended. A workspace that closed with its only member's account has no owner left to restore it, and only an operator's hold keeps it. Evidence outlives a deletion for as long as an operator holds it. Nobody gets a "delete now".
- People keep reading a deleted workspace until it goes, change nothing in it, and nothing in it keeps working or spending meanwhile.
- A stolen session proves nothing by itself: it can't delete without the mailbox, mint a fresher session, add a password, or try passwords past the proof's 5. Each code it asks for lands in the person's mailbox, which says what it would delete.
- Whoever holds the account's mailbox can delete with a session, as they could already reset its password.
- A self-hosted server without email keeps the password as the proof, and an account there without one (a social sign-in, the desktop app's owner) adds a password first, which takes a sign-in in the last 10 minutes.
- An account signed in with Apple behind "Hide My Email" has a `privaterelay.appleid.com` address, and Apple's relay passes on only mail from senders registered for that app (Apple Developer, Sign in with Apple for Email Communication, with SPF and DKIM passing). Without `EMAIL_FROM` registered there it gets no code, so it can't be deleted, and no other email from the server either.
- Old phone builds send no code: they show their generic failure, so the phone build that asks for the code ships with this server. A new build on an older server deletes as the old ones did.
- A hold that lands after the purge removed sign-ins and revoked grants keeps a workspace that lost them: its date had passed.
- A workspace goes in one cascading delete, under the job's renewed lease, and `context_usage` is read whole once per purge (no index on its `org_id`). Batch the delete by table, or index that column, if a very large one ever outlives the lease.
- Rolling the server back during a grace reopens closing workspaces (older code ignores the columns), and older workers fail `org.delete`, `user.files.purge` and the new emails for good. After the next deploy the hourly sweep finishes the purges; the emails queued meanwhile are lost.
- Support cases have no operator route: a blocked account, one whose address never reaches its owner (a mistyped one gets no code, nor the link that confirms it), the only owner of a suspended workspace with others in it, and erasure requests are handled by hand.

## Alternatives considered

- **Deleting at once, or a shorter window for an empty or unfinished workspace.** No runs doesn't mean no content, and people delete the wrong workspace alone too. One rule and one constant keep trust's window everywhere.
- **Closed during the 7 days** (built first, until 2026-11-11). It reused the suspension's enforcement and needed no read-only mode, but a screen of its own stood in the workspace's place for the 7 days. Read-only keeps the normal app and still stops all work and spending.
- **Fully usable until the date.** Open. Its work stops either way, so read-only is the default: what people wrote meanwhile would reach no agent.
- **No grace for accounts** (built first, until 2026-11-05). Restoring one by hand after a mistake meant recreating it under its old id with no sign-in method left. Apple accepts a process that takes a reasonable time when the person is told how long and gets a confirmation when it's done.
- **A sign-in in the last 10 minutes, or the password, as the proof** (built first). Two proofs, and a "sign in again" round trip for accounts without a password: the emailed code is one proof, and the person already reads that mailbox to reset a password. Telling a recent sign-in by the session's start, carried across minted sessions, stays: adding a password and the desktop hand-off need it (§2).
- **A column for the last real sign-in.** Keeping the source's sign-in time on a minted session gives the same guarantee, without a column or a hook on every sign-in.
- **A hold that refuses the deletion.** It would tell the workspace about a silent hold. Making the purge wait keeps the evidence and says nothing.
- **A column for "being purged".** Making the date the point of no return gives the same guarantee.
- **Disconnecting what a person added when they go.** Keys, connections and mailboxes are the workspace's, and removing them would break the team's agents.
- **Deleting a person's messages with their account.** They are the workspace's record; the dialog says they stay without the name.
- **A hand-over of the server's ownership, or refusing the pinned owner.** ADR 0073 §7 lets the owner go with the environment keeping control; a hand-over waits until self-hosters ask for one.
- **Operator routes to delete or restore workspaces and delete accounts.** By hand until the first real request.
- **Deleting abandoned onboardings after a while.** Not now: where new workspaces wait for their first plan, one waiting is no computer request (ADR 0079 §2), so one left behind costs little.
- **Checking the typed name on the server.** The proof guards the server; the name only stops a wrong pick in the one app that deletes.

## Addendum (2026-10-31): connectors through Composio

Amended by ADR 0107. The purge (`revokeGrants`) also deletes the workspace's connector accounts at Composio (`composio_account_id`, disconnected ones included), and the account of every Connect Link it made for one (`oauth_states`), never revoking them there: Composio's Google and Microsoft apps may share one grant with another workspace's connection of the same account. They go before the mailboxes, and a mailbox's grant (§3) also stays while a live connector on the same provider's app, of that account or of one it can't name, may share it. Each is marked as it goes; one Composio refuses keeps the workspace for the next try, as a mailbox does.
