# 0079. Billing hooks: billing holds, limits and a signed link

- Status: Accepted. Amended 2026-10-30 by ADR 0088 (§2: a workspace being deleted waits for no plan, and restoring one keeps the rule). Amended 2026-11-06 (§6, §7: the wall is the first send to an agent, and a key is no way around a hold)
- Date: 2026-10-27
- Extends: ADR 0002 (the core knows no plans), ADR 0056 (operator API, standing, external provisioning)
- Code: `packages/engine/src/standing.ts` (`BILLING_HOLDS`, `setBillingHold`), `packages/engine/src/organizations.ts` (`createOrganization`, `manage_billing`), `packages/engine/src/billing-link.ts`, `packages/engine/src/limits.ts`, `apps/server/src/routes/app/billing.ts`, `apps/server/src/operator/limits.ts`, `packages/client/src/lib/billing.ts`, `apps/web/src/components/BillingLinkButton.tsx`, `apps/web/src/routes/org.$orgId.onboarding.tsx`

## Context

Whoever runs an instance for paying customers (agentes.chat Cloud is one) needs four things from the server: a workspace that hasn't paid has to stop its agents, what a plan includes (people, files, the platform's own AI) has to be enforced, its owners need a way to pay, and none of it may change a self-hosted server. ADR 0002 rules out plans, prices or a payment processor inside the server process: a separate billing service owns them, and the operator's own services apply its decisions through the operator API (ADR 0056 §7, pulled, never pushed).

ADR 0056 already gives every hook but one: a hold stops a workspace's agents and keeps its people reading and writing, which is exactly what an unpaid workspace needs. What it lacks is a way to tell a hold for payment apart from a hold for review (they must never undo each other, and people must read different words), numbers to enforce, and a way into the billing service that proves who is asking.

## Decision

### 1. Billing holds are standing holds with a code

- A **billing hold** is a `hold` with the notice `shown` whose reason is one of three codes: `payment` (no plan yet), `unpaid` (its payment failed) and `ended` (its plan ended). Every other hold, and every suspension, is a **trust hold**.
- They stop exactly what any hold stops (ADR 0056 §8): the Model Gateway, new computer work, the judge and the router, routines and invitations. People still read and write, and a held workspace is never a computer request (ADR 0056 §10).
- They are set and lifted only through `PUT /api/operator/v1/orgs/{orgId}/billing-hold` with `{ hold: payment | unpaid | ended | null }`, which answers `{ applied, standing }`. It never replaces nor lifts a trust hold or a suspension (`applied: false`, as when it is already so), while a hold set through `PUT …/standing` replaces a billing hold as it replaces any hold: the billing service puts its own back once the trust hold is lifted. A hold set there with one of these reasons and the notice shown reads as a billing hold, so the operator's own tools leave the codes to the billing service.
- `setBillingHold` writes through the same path as `setOrgStanding`, in one transaction on the workspace's locked row: the audit row, the dropped computer wait, the `org.standing` event, the cache and the notification to owners and admins on a move. The notification carries the code (`latest.billingHold`) in place of a reason, and every app says each code in the person's words (`standing.billing.*`), never as it comes.

### 2. Creating a workspace

- Billing holds on a person's workspaces never stop them creating another, with one exception: owning a workspace on a `payment` hold refuses a new one with `org.create_unpaid` (403, `params.orgId` the oldest such workspace), so a person has at most one workspace waiting for its first plan. The check runs in the create's transaction under a lock per person, so two creates at once start one. The web app sends them to that workspace's plan instead, and the phone app only says that workspace isn't active yet.
- A trust hold or a suspension on any workspace they are in still refuses (`org.create_refused`), and so does a blocked account.
- With `BILLING_HOLD_NEW_WORKSPACES=true` a new workspace starts on a `payment` hold, written in the row's insert: no audit row and no notification, since nothing moved. The server refuses to start with it unless `BILLING_URL`, `BILLING_LINK_SECRET` and `OPERATOR_API_TOKEN` are set, because only the billing service lifts it. The computer provisioning keeps one reason (`capacity`): a workspace waiting for payment simply isn't a request.
- *Amended 2026-10-30 (ADR 0088):* a workspace being deleted waits for no plan. `org.create_unpaid` ignores it, so deleting the workspace that waits lets its owner make another at once. Restoring a workspace that waits for its first plan is refused with `org.create_unpaid` (`params.orgId`, the other one) while the person restoring owns another that waits, under the same lock per person as a create, so a person still has at most one. A trust hold or a suspension on a workspace being deleted still refuses a new one (`org.create_refused`). Billing holds never keep a deletion waiting past its date, and the billing service reads the deletion's events (ADR 0056 §5): on agentes.chat Cloud the plan stops renewing when the deletion is asked for and renews again at a restore.

### 3. A signed link to the billing service

- `POST /api/app/organizations/{orgId}/billing-link` with `{ from }` answers `{ url, expiresAt }` for owners and admins (`manage_billing`); a suspended workspace refuses it (`org.suspended`), a held one doesn't, since paying is how a billing hold ends, nor one being deleted (ADR 0088 §3), whose payer still reaches its invoices and card. Without `BILLING_URL` it answers 404 `billing.off`, and `GET /api/app/auth-config` says `billingUrl: null`.
- The URL is `BILLING_URL/link?t=<token>`, with `token = "v1." + payload + "." + signature`: the payload is the claims as UTF-8 JSON in base64url, the signature the base64url HMAC-SHA256 of `"v1." + payload` keyed with `BILLING_LINK_SECRET`. The billing service checks the HMAC over the payload exactly as received (constant time) before it parses anything.
- The claims: `aud: "agentes-billing"`, a random `jti`, `iat` and `exp` (seconds, `exp = iat + 300`), the workspace's id and name, the person's id, email, name, role (`owner` or `admin`) and language, `from` (`settings`, `hold`, `onboarding`, `new_workspace`, `invite_full`, `upload_full`, `storage`: the billing service opens on what it came for), `return` (the path on this server it sends the person back to) and `usage`: people, agents, stored bytes (null on the workspace's own bucket), `messages` (in every conversation a person can read, agent-only rooms left out), `channels` (named and not archived), `files` (Drive's, the trash left out), `wikiPages`, `issues` (not canceled) and `since` (when the workspace was created, in seconds). Counts only: never a name or a word of content, never per channel or per person. Messages are counted per conversation on an index without reading one (ADR 0009), so a thread's first message deleted before any reply still counts.
- `return` is always an app path under the workspace, `/org/{orgId}` or one of its pages: `/settings/billing` for `settings`, `/settings/storage` for `storage`, `/members` for `invite_full`, and `/onboarding` for `onboarding`, the onboarding's computer step. That one opens the onboarding again at the first step still waiting for the person: the furthest step the server keeps, or further where the workspace shows it was passed (its team made, with the import picked before it in; then, with the workspace's own computer and nothing held over it, the computer step too), and the AI step for a workspace with no step kept. The web app reads the workspace, its context and its agents back from the server (the tab keeps only which agents the import made, and their models), and says the plan is active only once nothing is held over the workspace. The person can land before the billing service lifts the `payment` hold, so the page asks again every few seconds until then. A workspace it can't read back opens as it is. The billing service sends people to `return` after Checkout, adding `?paid=1` when the payment went through. That is the address's word, not the server's: until the hold lifts, the hold banner says the payment arrived, asking again every few seconds too, and the onboarding says the payment is being confirmed, with the computer step's offer held back, for a minute at most; then the server's standing decides, with the offer back while the workspace still waits for its plan. The billing service accepts only such paths, on this server. Since 2026-11-03 the web app also sends the page the link was opened from (`returnTo`): when it is one of this workspace's pages (`/org/{orgId}/…`, lowercase letters, digits, `-` and `/` only, since the billing service refuses the whole link over any other `return`), `return` is that page's path, without its search or hash, instead of the entry point's default, so paying from a channel (an upload past the limit, the hold banner) lands back in that channel. Anything else (another workspace, another host, the onboarding outside the workspace) is ignored.
- The billing service accepts 30 seconds of clock skew, refuses a link that claims more than 10 minutes, and accepts each `jti` once. The web app mints the link on the click and only ever navigates to it, so it lands in no history or log of ours.
- `BILLING_LINK_SECRET` (at least 32 characters) is shared with the billing service. Rotating it means setting the new value on both sides together: links minted just before fail once and are minted again.

### 4. Four limits, null meaning none

`PUT /api/operator/v1/orgs/{orgId}/limits` sets `{ members, membersMax, payerUserId, storageBytes, uploadBytesPerDay, platformUsdPerMonth }`, each a number or null (no limit; `membersMax` and `payerUserId` may be left out, as null); `GET /orgs/{orgId}` answers them with `updatedAt`, and the meters. A change (never the same limits set again) sends the workspace's apps `org.limits`, with no payload, and they read their limits again, so a new plan shows within seconds. A self-hosted server never sets any, so nothing is enforced there.

| Limit | Counts | Enforced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `members` | every membership row, blocked accounts included; never agents, invitations or requests to join | when someone joins (accepting an invitation, an approved request), under the workspace's `org-members` lock, so it holds exactly; and before an invitation is sent, resent or a request approved, so a full workspace sends none that can't be accepted (`org.members_full`, 409) |
| `membersMax` | not a limit: the most people the plan takes with seats added. `GET /api/app/organizations/{orgId}/limits` answers it as `members.max`, with `members.canAddSeats` below it. In a full workspace, whoever manages billing reads that a seat can be added in Plan and billing, or at the top that the plan can change; anyone else reads to ask them. The people meter counts against it, with the seats paid for under it | never |
| `payerUserId` | not a limit: the user id of whoever pays for the plan, the only one the billing service lets add seats or change plans (it refuses anyone else with `not_payer`). Stored as `organizations.payer_user_id`, with no foreign key: an id not in the workspace means no one here pays. The app's limits answer it per viewer as `members.payer`, `{ you, name }` or null when not said; `name` only to anyone else who manages billing, while the payer is in the workspace. In a full workspace the payer reads the words above; anyone else who manages billing reads "Ask {{payer}}, who pays for this workspace, to add a seat" (or to switch plans at the top), "whoever pays" without a name; members read as before. Not said (a billing service older than it): everyone who manages billing reads the words above | never |
| `storageBytes` | the bytes of the files a workspace keeps on this server's storage, files in the trash left out | before any file is written (chat and Drive uploads, a computer's uploads, mail attachments, every version, skill files), as `used + incoming > limit` (`storage.full`, 409); skipped for a workspace on its own bucket. Also before a file comes back from the trash and before files are copied back from the workspace's own bucket, which would otherwise bring back more than the limit |
| `uploadBytesPerDay` | the bytes written to that storage in the last 24 hours, rolling | where files are written (`storage.daily_full`, 409); never for files that come back, which keep the day they were first written |
| `platformUsdPerMonth` | usage billed to the platform this UTC calendar month | §5 |

Bytes count in GiB (1024³), the unit the apps print. The storage check takes no lock: uploads at the same moment may pass the line by at most their own sizes, where a lock would serialize every upload of a workspace. The refusals tell agents in plain English what happened and what a person can do.

### 5. The platform's own AI, capped per month

- Over `platformUsdPerMonth`, the platform's judge (Jev) is skipped and the workspace's own cheap model answers in its place, the path a Jev outage already takes; what only Jev does (images and boxes for computer use) fails like an outage. The router starts no run (`platform_cap`, stored like `no_ai`, before any step of the chain of ADR 0074), and the app says to mention an agent with @, which still starts it.
- The spend is read from usage records with a 60-second cache, so it may run up to a minute of calls past the cap.
- The operator's events feed gets `platform_ai.threshold` (`{ month, percent: 50 | 100, spentUsd, limitUsd }`) the first time a month's spend reaches each threshold, once per workspace, month and threshold.

### 6. What people see

- The server's words name no plan, price or currency. A billing hold says its code's words, and owners and admins get the button that fixes it on the billing service; other members read that whoever runs the workspace can. Settings gets "Plan and billing" for owners and admins only where `billingUrl` is set.
- A plan a hold stops says so where it would sign in, on every plan and every screen that offers one (`planGate`'s `billing`, `PlanHold`): owners and admins get the button, others read that whoever runs the workspace can. *Amended 2026-11-06:* no screen offers an API key on a hold, since a hold stops the Model Gateway, keys included: Connect shows `PlanHold` in place of its picker, wherever it was opened from, and Settings › keys says keys open with the plan (`connectAi.key.closed`). `createCredential` and `rotateCredential` refuse too (`credentials.plan_hold`, 403). A key saved before the hold stays listed, and can only be removed. A self-hosted server never gets this (`planHoldOf`, which counts a hold until the edition is known, so no moment of Connect shows first).
- The phone app shows the words and never a button or a link to pay: the stores allow paying only through their own purchase flow. The desktop app loads the site and gets the site's buttons. A full workspace's note on the phone never names Plan and billing, a seat to add or a plan to change (App Store 3.1.1): on a server with a billing service it says only that every paid seat is taken and, to anyone but the payer, to talk to whoever pays (`membersFullWhoPhone`); without one, the words of every app.

### 7. The wall (2026-11-06)

An unpaid workspace runs no agent and makes no model call, with or without a key. It meets the wall at the first send that would start an agent: a DM, an @mention, or a channel message in a channel with agents. Everything before it is open and costs no model call: Lodi's DM, the team templates, the team card, Criar o time, agents, channels, Drive, the wiki and setting up routines.

- **The run.** On a billing hold `executeRun` ends with `no_ai` and the agent's card with `reason: plan_hold`, no judge read included (`postNoAiCard`). Trust holds end as before, saying why, and so does what has no person's message to keep: background work and one agent's call to another. The card is `KeptCard` on the web and the phone, and every no-AI card on a hold reads as it, so a Connect from before leads nowhere. On `unpaid` and `ended` it says the hold's own words and button.
- **Keeping the message.** A `no_ai` run can be restarted, which is why the card goes down that path: after the plan and an AI, the same message is answered. `answerNow` refuses first while a workspace may not work (`assertCanWork`), since a key saved earlier reads as ready. A second message within the card's 6 hours gets no card, and its receipt reads "Guardada". A channel message nobody mentioned, on `payment` and with agents there, is stored as a `no_ai` decision (`routeMessage`, `held`), and its receipt says so with the plan's button (`kept`, from the decision's `strategy`). With no agent there nothing is stored, and on `unpaid` and `ended` the router skips as before.
- **After paying.** `keptCardState`: the wall; back with `?paid=1` the button hides and the banner says the payment arrived, on every route (`usePaidReturn` reads the address itself and keeps it for the tab); with the hold lifted and no AI, Connect for the whole team; with an AI, connected from anywhere, opening the conversation starts the answer with no click, and if that start is refused "Responder agora" stays. Agents called together share a card, and every one of them answers (`answeringRuns`). A `kept` channel message is routed when its author opens the conversation. The `computer_ready` email's button opens the conversation with the newest kept message (`keptUrl`).
- **Lodi's DM.** `introCards` and `offersTeamSetup` take `held`: no Connect, no style card and no "Montar o time". Amended 2026-11-07 (ADR 0155 §6): the owner's first-run checklist holds the setup. Rows 1 and 2 stay open on a hold (the context is saved and "Criar o time" makes a template's team at once, with no model call), the AI row leads to the plan (words only on the phone), apps and people read "Abre com o plano." with no button, and its asks wait for the plan. A saved key reads as ready, so the hold is checked first. The tap kept as a message (`meta.templateTap`) and the 30-day card are gone. Primeira tarefa shows a labelled example for the template's first agent (`params.example`), never posted.
- **The banner.** On `payment`: none while the workspace has only its right hand, then one line with the plan's button. `unpaid` and `ended` keep theirs.
- **Also closed.** Invitations (the form gives way to a line), Rodar agora and a routine's webhook Testar (refused at once, since a slot on hold passes silently), embeddings (`gateway.embed` answers null, so search falls back to full text).
- **Coming back.** `queuePlanWaiting`, on the first team or kept message (a channel's included), queues `plan.waiting` an hour and three days later, deduped per workspace and step. The job checks at send: still on `payment`, to whoever may `manage_billing`, at a confirmed address. Its link is `/org/<id>/settings/billing`, not an app link, so a phone opens the website, the one place it can pay.
- **The phone** shows the same cards, in words only: "Choose one on the web" for whoever pays, who can for anyone else (§6).

The words name no price (§6). A self-hosted server never gets any of it: holds don't exist without `BILLING_URL`, and the screens go through `planHoldOf`.

## Consequences

- A self-hosted server changes nothing: without `BILLING_URL` there is no billing anywhere, and without limits nothing is enforced.
- A trust hold put over a billing hold, then lifted, leaves the workspace active until the billing service sets its hold again, so the operator's services re-sync billing whenever they lift a hold.
- The link is a credential for five minutes. It is minted only on a click, carried only in the address bar, and used once by the billing service.
- One secret signs every link, with no overlap when it changes.
- Billing's numbers reach the server only through the operator API: when the billing service is down, workspaces keep their last holds and limits.

## Amendment (2026-11-08): the plan picked in the app, and one way to the plan per place (§3, §7)

ADR 0162: the billing link may carry `plan`, the billing service's own key for the plan picked in the onboarding,
passed on unread so the billing service opens that plan's Checkout. The plan's name and price come to the browser from
the billing service's public `plans.json`, never from this server. On a hold, the hold's banner is the workspace's one
way to the plan: the right hand's card and the first-task card offer none.
